What is UCD?

Definition

User-Centered Design (UCD) means designing a system, app, or product based on what the USERS need — not just what the developer or company wants. The user is always at the center, and every decision is based on user needs and feedback.

"Good design = based on the NEEDS and WANTS of USERS"

— Donald Norman, pioneer of User-Centered Design

UCD Methodology

  • UCD is NOT a one-time design — it is a continuous, iterative process.
  • It involves the involvement of users throughout the design process.
  • It relies on the use of an iterative design cycle — design, test, improve, repeat.
  • UCD is research-based, not guessing — it's about HOW we gather information about users.

Six Phases of UCD

1
Specify the use context and users' needs
2
Specify business requirements
3
Build design solutions from rough concept to finished design
4
Evaluate designs with usability testing
5
Implementation — develop and deliver the product
6
Deployment — final product evaluated as consumer needs change

Key Aspects of UCD

1
Early focus on users, tasks, and environments
2
Active involvement of users
3
Appropriate allocation of function between user and system
4
Iterative design — prototype is designed, tested, and modified
5
Multi-disciplinary design teams

Responsive Principles of UCD

  • Clear understanding of the users' tasks and environments
  • Evaluation-driven design — decisions driven by usability testing
  • Considering the general consumer experience — the full journey matters
  • Involving the client within the design process — collaboration is key

Impacts & Opportunities of UCD

UCD starts with PEOPLE

  • Their needs
  • Their feelings — hopes and fears
  • Their real-life problems

"UCD has a human bias in its DNA"

Four Essential Activities — ISO 13407

Activity Description
1. Requirements Gathering Understanding and specifying the context of use — who the users are, what they do, and in what environment.
2. Requirements Specification Specifying the user and organizational requirements — what the system must do to meet user needs.
3. Design Producing designs and prototypes — moving from concepts to tangible, testable solutions.
4. Evaluation Carrying out user-based assessment of the site or system — testing with real users and iterating based on findings.
